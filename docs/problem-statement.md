# Problem Statement

## The Specific Audience Affected
This problem affects logistics coordinators, supply chain managers, and fleet operators who are responsible for transporting high-value or time-sensitive cargo. This includes pharmaceutical companies transporting vaccines, food distributors handling perishables, and tech companies moving sensitive electronics.

## Why Existing Solutions Don't Solve It
Current supply chain management software is highly fragmented. A logistics manager typically has one screen open for GPS fleet tracking, another screen for weather forecasts, and a third screen for IoT temperature sensors. 
During a crisis (like a sudden port strike or extreme weather event), humans cannot manually cross-reference these systems fast enough. By the time a human realizes a specific truck is in the path of a storm, the delay has already occurred.

## Quantified Pain
- **Financial Loss:** A single temperature excursion across any leg of a cold-chain journey can spoil an entire cargo load, costing upwards of **$500,000 per incident**.
- **Macro Impact:** The 2021 LA/Long Beach port backlog resulted in over 100 ships waiting offshore for weeks, costing global supply chains an estimated **$10 Billion**.
- **Time Lost:** DevOps and logistics teams suffer from severe alert fatigue, spending 3+ hours per incident manually investigating root causes.

## Why This Problem Matters Now
Geopolitical instability and climate change are severely increasing the frequency of global supply chain disruptions. The industry can no longer rely on reactive tracking; it requires proactive, AI-driven resilience to ensure critical goods (like medicine and food) reach their destinations safely.
